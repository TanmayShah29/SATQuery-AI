# SIH 2026: Problem Statement Selection & Scoring Framework

Selecting the right problem statement is the single most critical strategic decision in the entire SIH cycle. Choosing the wrong statement results in competing against 600+ teams on a generic problem or hitting an insurmountable hardware/data roadblock.

---

## 1. The 5-Point Evaluation Matrix

Evaluate candidate problem statements from `https://www.sih.gov.in/sih2026PS` using this 100-point rubric:

| Dimension | Weight | Scoring Guideline |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Submission Volume / Competition Density** | 25 pts | Check the *Submitted Idea(s) Count* column on the portal. Statements with <50 submissions score 25; statements with >300 submissions score 5. Lower competition drastically raises finalist odds. |
| **2. 36-Hour Prototype Feasibility** | 25 pts | Can a functional, impressive end-to-end prototype be completed in 36 hours? If the project requires collecting 50,000 real physical samples or months of model training, score low. If it leverages APIs, pretrained models, and modern frameworks, score high. |
| **3. Sponsoring Ministry Clarity** | 20 pts | Does the statement specify concrete inputs, outputs, and department workflows? Problem statements with vague 2-line descriptions lead to subjective jury scoring. Detailed requirements lead to objective wins. |
| **4. Team Skill Match & Tech Stack Alignment** | 15 pts | Does the team already have 70% of the foundational skills required (e.g., computer vision, full-stack, mobile, cloud)? |
| **5. Demo Wow-Factor** | 15 pts | Does the solution allow an interactive visual demo (interactive dashboard, mobile app, map visualization, or real-time simulation) that engages judges in 60 seconds? |

---

## 2. Selection Strategy: Student Innovation vs. Ministry Specific

1. **Ministry Problem Statements (Recommended)**:
   - High prize prestige, direct interaction with government officers, potential for real adoption.
   - Sponsoring ministries frequently fund follow-up implementation pilots through AICTE/MIC grants.
2. **Student Innovation / Open Category**:
   - Extremely high competition volume.
   - Requires extraordinary novelty to clear initial screening.
